## 1. What it is

A GB10 runs its graphics cores as fast as its power budget and temperature allow. Under sustained load that is about 2.40-2.46 GHz, even though `nvidia-smi` advertises a 3,003 MHz maximum. When the chip or the box around it gets hot, or when it bumps into its internal power limit, firmware briefly slows the clock (throttling, a forced temporary slowdown); if the hottest part of the box keeps climbing, the embedded controller (EC, the board's own firmware) cuts power without writing a log. An operator can put a ceiling on the clock with one root command, `nvidia-smi -lgc 0,<MHz>` (a clock cap, a fixed upper limit). Whether that ceiling pays off depends on the phase of work. Prefill (reading the whole prompt) is mostly arithmetic, so it slows roughly in step with the clock: about 1.5-2.5% per 100 MHz below 2.4 GHz. Decode (writing one token at a time) mostly waits on memory, so at low concurrency it barely notices a 10% clock cut, but the cost grows with batch size. On one node, a memory-bound test kernel lost nothing under a 2,200 MHz ceiling while a compute-bound one lost about 10%. In return a cap of 2,100-2,200 MHz takes 7-12 °C off peak GPU temperature and a quarter to a half off GPU power, and it removes the throttle episodes that otherwise start within a single deep prompt. On a multi-node model, where one model is split across nodes (tensor parallel), every node waits for the slowest one at each layer, so all nodes must carry the same cap, and the slowest node is not always the hottest one, so it has to be measured. The trouble is that the tools most people watch cannot see the effect: the temperature `nvidia-smi` reports runs 7-17 °C below the zone the EC acts on, the power it reports covers only the GPU (a fifth to a half of what the box draws from the wall, depending on load), and a dashboard sampling every few seconds cannot catch individual clock clamps. What does show them is a set of cumulative stopwatch counters that have to be compared before and after each test. This page covers what a cap costs and saves on each workload, what the throttle and power-off bands look like, what the telemetry can and cannot see, and the procedure for A/B-testing a clock change without confounding the benchmark. See also [Prefill optimization](04-prefill-optimization.md), [Decode optimization](05-decode-optimization.md), [Inter-Spark communication](03-inter-spark-communication.md), [Profiling protocol](14-profiling-protocol.md) and [SM121 hardware facts](17-sm121-hardware-facts.md).

## 2. How each engine does it

No engine in scope reads or sets GPU clocks, power limits, temperatures or clock-event reasons. On GB10 the clock is a host-level driver setting applied by root with `nvidia-smi -lgc`, outside any engine, and every engine runs at whatever the driver allows. Engines matter to this topic only through the heat and power transients they generate (prefill chunk size, batch size, CPU busy-waits) and, for ds4, an optional duty cycle. [Code-verified: tree greps at each pin, excluding vendored NVML bindings]

### vLLM

- Mechanism. NVML use in `vllm/platforms/cuda.py` @ 34b069080d9f is limited to device handles, compute capability, UUID, memory info and related device queries. A tree grep finds no clock, power, temperature, clock-event-reason or `-lgc` call. Defaults on CC 12.1: the engine inherits the driver clock, which is about 2.40-2.46 GHz under sustained load at stock (§3). [Code-verified]
- CPU heat on the tensor-parallel head node. `vllm/distributed/device_communicators/shm_broadcast.py:112` defines `class SpinCondition`; line 133 sets `busy_loop_s: float = 1`, and lines 202-203 call `sched_yield()`, so for one second after each shared-memory read a reader spins instead of blocking. [Code-verified] AlexLJC measured this spin at 1.924 CPU cores on the head node of a two-node GX10 TP=2 DeepSeek V4 Flash 0731 lane. A 250 µs bounded sleep cut it to 0.025 cores, cut the head-minus-worker SoC (TSOC) temperature gap from 10.27 to 7.42 °C at matched GPU power, cut head TSOC p95 by 5.20 °C, and cut time at or above 95 °C from 8.25% to 1.02%. The author states the change "does not establish a throughput speedup or regression". [Community-measured, AlexLJC/deepseek-v4-flash-gb10 @ 096c42d spincondition report] A GPU clock cap does not touch this heat source. [Interpretation]
- A second source for the spin heat. nacyot (ASUS GX10, vLLM TP=2) changed the same `busy_loop_s` default from 1 s to 2 ms: combined vLLM CPU fell from 333% to 89% and the SoC zone by 11 °C, and in a reproduction the default reached 90 °C at 2 min 18 s and peaked at 95 °C against 85 °C patched. The same write-up reports that a later re-verification found single-stream decode about 2-6% slower with the patch (38.7 vs 40.9 tok/s, inside run-to-run variance but consistent in direction; no difference at 4 streams), so the author treats the patch as a switch for hot conditions. [Community-measured, nacyot node-setup write-up §2.3]
- Aborts. vLLM notices a client abort only after prefill completes, so a runaway deep prefill cannot be cancelled to shed heat. Gate the request before it starts. [Historical diagnostic, 2026-08-16 GX10 fleet serving bake-off]
- Metrics for weighting a cap decision. Mean prompt size is `vllm:request_prompt_tokens_sum / vllm:request_prompt_tokens_count`. The prefill share of time is `vllm:time_to_first_token_seconds_sum / vllm:e2e_request_latency_seconds_sum`. The same `e2e_request_latency_seconds_sum` is the load-seconds denominator for throttle counters. [Community-measured, agjs/gb10-clock-cap @ c3cd062 `AGENTS.md` lines 26, 43, 74]
- Cap effects on other vLLM topologies. neko-legends (4× DGX Spark, vLLM TP4, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash with DSpark): "Unlocked DVFS dips to ~1970 MHz under sustained prefill; the lock holds ~2171", decode unchanged ("not clock-bound"), and "same throughput as a 2400 lock at ~21% fewer watts". There is no per-arm table, so it stays out of the §4 table. [Community-measured, neko-legends/spark-bench @ b46eabcb README line 1094] The same README's cold 32K prefill "~950 -> ~2100 tok/s" spans other changes and is not a cap effect (§7).
- Kernel-level mechanism under a resident vLLM job. On one Founders Edition node with a vLLM TP8 job resident and idle, a `-lgc 0,2200` lock left a bandwidth-bound decode-shaped GEMV unchanged and cut a compute-bound matmul by about 10% (§4). [Community-measured, im0xMagnus on tonyd2wild issue #1]
- A hidden slow state on older firmware stacks cost vLLM TP4 serving about 63 vs 94 ms per decode step while clocks, reason bits and counters looked normal (§3, §4). [Community-measured]
- Anemll's DSpark images and the eugr nightly images are vLLM builds; their clock behaviour is the above. Most cap A/Bs in §4 are vLLM lanes; the exceptions are the diffusion, cuBLAS, kernel-probe and llama.cpp rows.

### SGLang

- Mechanism. There is no clock, power or thermal code at @ 2909f84e3b2f. NVML is used only for P2P status (`custom_all_reduce_utils.py`), compute capability (`utils/common.py`), GPU fabric info (`utils/cuda_vmm_utils.py`) and NUMA memory affinity (`utils/numa_utils.py`). [Code-verified]
- `--sleep-on-idle` ("Reduce CPU usage when sglang is idle", default False, `srt/arg_groups/fields/device.py:81`) installs an `IdleSleeper` only on the scheduler with PP rank 0, attention-TP rank 0 and CP rank 0 (`srt/managers/scheduler.py:828-841`). Its docstring cites "more CPU thermal headroom". No GB10 measurement of it exists. [Code-verified]
- The only sustained SGLang thermal record on GB10 (griffith.mark, forum 348760 #64). One DGX Spark, stock cooling, ~22 °C ambient; SGLang serving Qwen3.5-4B with NEXTN speculative decoding at 2-12 concurrent requests, ~95% SoC utilisation for hours, with three more models resident and idle; thermal zones sampled by a user watchdog polling every 60 s. With all four models resident, c=12 held zones 0/1/5 at 97 °C, c=6 reached 93 °C in ~3 min, and c=2 reached 94 °C, while `nvidia-smi` read the GPU at 65-78 °C, 15-20 °C below the SoC zone. Every tier hit the author's own 92 °C kill threshold (a watchdog, not EC behaviour). After the three idle containers were stopped (~32 GB freed), c=6 peaked at 83 °C and throughput roughly doubled (1.7 -> 3.4 rows/s). The author reports fan and ambient unchanged but gives no log, and the arms ran for very different lengths, so the memory-residency explanation is a hypothesis (§7, §8). No cap A/B, and no measurement of `--sleep-on-idle`. [Community-measured]
- No published SGLang cap A/B on GB10. Own SGLang lanes at stock held the SM clock at or above 2,392 MHz with no throttling and 71-76 °C peaks (2026-09-15 DeepSeek V4.1 Flash TP3 k3 and k5 scorecards). A 2200 MHz guard on a single-node lane held 2,177-2,184 MHz (2026-08-19 single-node Qwen 27B NVFP4 fast lane). Neither was an A/B. [Measured, no A/B]

### llama.cpp and forks

- llama.cpp @ d2e54583c745: no NVML use and no clock or thermal code under `ggml/`, `src/` or `tools/`. [Code-verified]
- The first llama.cpp cap sweep on GB10 (nacyot; one ASUS GX10; llama-server; DeepSeek V4 Flash UD-Q2_K_XL; warm-up, then 3 × 256-token decode, median; GPU package power as the driver reports it, sampled at 1 Hz). Decode went from 17.47 tok/s uncapped (~2,496 MHz measured) to 17.05 at 2200 (2,185 MHz measured, −2.4%), 16.67 at 2000 (1,964 MHz, −4.6%) and 16.24 at 1800 (1,768 MHz, −7.0%). Over the same steps power fell 44.8 -> 27.6 -> 22.3 -> 19.0 W and GPU temperature 68.5 -> 56.8 -> 54.5 -> 52.7 °C. Requests of 2600 MHz or more left the clock at ~2,495 MHz and matched the uncapped arm. [Community-measured, §4]
- Wall and rail at a cap under llama.cpp prompt processing (vasimv, forum 373199 #22; one ASUS GX10; Qwen3.6-27B; wall meter at 120 V; rail read from nvtop): stock drew 85-95 W on the rail and up to 180 W at the wall, and `-lgc 200,2100` drew 55 W and 125 W. Throughput per arm is not given. [Community-measured]
- Stock llama.cpp power and heat. A GX10 token-per-watt suite read 32.6-44.3 W on the GPU rail across 3B-32B Q4_K_M and Q8_0 models (pendakwahteknologi/gx10-benchmarks @ 20edc947; nvidia-smi every 0.5 s; 512 tokens × 5). A ~45-minute llama.cpp run on four OEM units held clock at ~80 °C and ~60-66 W rail with no throttling (Alex Ziskind video). A GX10 under long-context llama.cpp inference read 2,379-2,392 MHz while the rail rose from 67 to 83 W and the GPU from 59 to 75 °C within ~65 s (forum 371402). [Community-measured]
- A closed-loop PID governor on the hottest ACPI zone has been run under llama.cpp (pju-hoge/gb10-thermal-governor @ 70ca6212); see §5, lever 9.
- ds4 duty cycle. antirez/ds4 @ 0aaea5a238fb has an engine-level `--power N` flag (1-100, default 100). It measures work time and sleeps `work * (100 - N) / N` after each prefill layer and after each decoded token, aiming for N% GPU occupancy. Work time is an exponential moving average (weights 0.875 / 0.125), not the last sample (`ds4.c:17826-17866`). The README (lines 211-212) says "V4.1 and GLM currently require `--power 100`". The Entrpi/ds4 fork @ b0a147a7fba6 keeps the same helpers (`ds4.h:142, 246`; `ds4.c:16073, 16497`). [Code-verified] No GB10 measurement of `--power` throughput, power or temperature exists. [Interpretation] Unlike `-lgc`, this lever needs no root and slows prefill and decode alike, so it costs decode throughput too.
- Entrpi/ds4-on-spark @ 99fb7b1: the installer queries only GPU name and compute capability; no clock handling. [Code-verified]
- Own single-node ds4-fork readouts at stock: 74 °C and 2,405 MHz through a maximum-effort reasoning cell, no thermal events (2026-08-17 reasoning-tax minibench, spark cells); peak 72 °C and 62.98 W GPU rail (2026-08-22 single-node DeepSeek V4 Flash agent-client operational). [Measured, no A/B]
- Community context: a llama-server Gemma 4 31B hard-freeze report logged SW-thermal bits nine times and a HW-thermal bit once at 74-85 °C GPU; no cap A/B (forum 383624 #1). The nacyot sweep above is the only published llama.cpp cap A/B on GB10, and no ds4-fork cap A/B exists. [Community-measured]

### ExLlamaV3 in vLLM

- ExLlamaV3 @ 6b84a21b6f1e: the `pynvml` init is commented out (`exllamav3/util/memory.py:9-11`); no clock code. [Code-verified]
- Cap evidence on EXL3-in-vLLM comes from two sources: MiaAI-Lab's two-node A/B/A (§4) and the own 2026-09-21 three-node sweep (§4). In a census confounded by memory pressure, a second two-node EXL3 kit recorded "GPU clocks stayed at 2,424 MHz with no throttle flags". [Community-measured, Entrpi/glm-5.3-flash-exl3-2x-spark @ 63f254f `docs/FINDINGS.md:657-660`]
- Hidden lock. MiaAI-Lab's commit 4bac6e26 reports that an `nvidia-clock-lock.service` had been enabled on both ranks since 2026-08-20, about 2.5 weeks before the commit. Every benchmark the kit published in that window was therefore measured capped, understating uncapped prefill by about 4%. [Community-measured, MiaAI-Lab/GLM-5.3-Flash-EXL3-2x-DGX-Sparks @ 4bac6e26 commit message]
- Per-rank telemetry on a three-node EXL3 TP=3 ring (NNNtrance, 3× ASUS GX10). After firmware levelling, sustained clocks were 2,485 / 2,502 / 2,440 MHz, a 2.5% spread, with memory bandwidth identical at 225-231 GB/s; the authors call it ordinary die-to-die binning. In an 85-minute 1M-needle run (141 samples per node) the head averaged 83.4 °C (87 °C peak) and the workers 79.7 and 82.0 °C, with SM clocks of 2,398 / 2,450 / 2,356 MHz: "it does not hold the lowest clock — worker-2 does". [Community-measured, NNNtrance/GLM-5.3-Flash-EXL3-DGX-Spark @ 7f1bd29 hardware doc §1.2 and CHANGELOG lines 306-314]
- The same repo disables its cap service on all three nodes, stating that "leaving one node capped costs all three 16%" and that the cap cost "15.9% of compute, zero of memory bandwidth". It gives no cap value, protocol or per-arm data, and its CHANGELOG uses 15.9% only as a boot-to-boot C8 spread with nothing changed, so the figure is not used as a cap cost (§7). The same section says the cap "had not prevented any of our crashes (all three were memory, not clocks)". [Community-measured, unverified figure]
- A single-node EXL3 recipe under a cap (nacyot; one GX10; DeepSeek V4 Flash REAP K216 at 3.0 bpw; cap ~1,989 MHz). Decode was 36-37 tok/s (512 × 3) and 38-44 tok/s (acceptance code), against 44-47 in the upstream README at a different context and output format. No power or temperature was reported for this recipe. The author suspects that EXL3 trellis decode is clock-sensitive but ran no uncapped A/B. [Community-measured, confounded; §8]

## 3. What is different on GB10 / SM121

- [Community-measured] The loaded clock at stock is about 2.40-2.46 GHz, not 3,003 MHz. agjs: "power-limited to ~2455 MHz in practice", with a recomputed median of 2,437-2,457 MHz across two nodes. hoesing (cuBLAS FP32 4096 matmul, 20 samples at 0.5 s): 2,424-2,483 MHz, average 2,446 MHz, 84.0 W average. tniccum's replacement Founders Edition pair settled at about 2,400 MHz under a 262K prefill with no slowdown flags. Own sweep: median 2,392-2,411 MHz [Measured]. "Max Clocks 3,003 MHz" and "Default Applications Clocks 2,418 MHz" are `nvidia-smi` readouts, not achieved clocks (forum 370304, driver 580.142; own captures on 580.173.02). Short bursts go higher: an unlocked Founders Edition node reached a 2,554 MHz maximum during a probe (im0xMagnus), and an NVFP4 benchmark author reached ~2.6 GHz, and the advertised sparse FP4 peak, only in short runs at factory settings, "settling around the 2.45Ghz typically observed" on longer ones (adg1, forum 377375 #21, #24). The advertised FP4 peak therefore needs a clock that no source shows GB10 sustaining. [Interpretation]
- [Community-measured] Raising the floor does not raise the sustained clock. With `-lgc 2418,3003` "the GPU still ran 2463–2496 MHz at 33 W, unchanged. It self-limits well below the 3003 MHz 'max'" (KamakuraCrypto, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next dual-Spark PR head b08d24a `autoresearch/RESULTS.md:238-239`; the benchmark behind those lines is not stated).
- [Community-measured] After `-lgc`, "Applications Clocks" and "Max Clocks" still read 2,418 and 3,003. The lock shows only in the current clock under load (`clocks.current.sm`, `nvidia-smi -q -d CLOCK`) (ai-muninn). Checking the wrong field makes a working lock look failed. On driver 580.173.02 a `-lgc 0,2200` lock takes effect while `clocks_event_reasons.applications_clocks_setting` reads "Not Active" (im0xMagnus, eight Founders Edition nodes), and no query returns the applied `-lgc` minimum and maximum (forum 280966, a desktop GPU; context). On driver 595.84, `nvidia-smi --query-gpu=clocks.applications.graphics` returns the string "Requested functionality has been deprecated", which breaks any health check that compares it as a number; read `clocks.sm` instead (nacyot).
- [Community-measured] The clock snaps to discrete steps, so a requested value is a target, not an exact setting. "Ask for 1200, get 1098" (peter.h177, relayed by an NVIDIA moderator, forum 377478 #30). `-lgc 1000,1000` held 955 MHz under matmul (forum 383624 #3). Own: 2200 gives 2,177-2,190 MHz, 2100 gives 2,080-2,086, 2000 gives 1,982, and 1900 gives 1,891 [Measured]. In nacyot's llama.cpp sweep, 2200 / 2000 / 1800 measured 2,185 / 1,964 / 1,768 MHz. GB10 accepts arbitrary integer MHz values even though `--query-supported-clocks` returns N/A (dev.to post; MiaAI-Lab harness line 4).
- [Community-measured] Every power limit reads N/A, so `nvidia-smi -pl` does nothing. `-lgc` is the only host-side clock or power lever. Also N/A: Shutdown T.Limit, Slowdown T.Limit, target temperature, memory temperature, memory clock, module power, memory power, and fan speed. "Power Samples" reads "Not Found", and Max Operating T.Limit reads 0 °C (forum 370304 #1; own 2026-08-19/20 captures [Historical diagnostic]). GPU power draw (average and instantaneous) is reported, and it covers the GPU only. NVIDIA staff: "When measuring power usage via NVIDIA-smi, the wattage displayed measures only GPU power" (forum 349668), and "there is no method to monitor CPU power and currently no plans to expose CPU rail information" (forum 360631 #3-4). The Grace tuning guide's GPU and module power-capping scopes (`nvidia-smi -pl <W> -sc 0|1`) and automatic power steering are documented for Grace Hopper and GB200, not for GB10 (fetched 2026-09-23).
- [Code-verified, vendor doc] Power envelope, as specifications and never as achieved figures. The NVIDIA support page gives a 140 W TDP "of the GB10 chip, including CPU and GPU". NVIDIA staff (forum 349668): peak total system power 240 W, GB10 SoC TDP 140 W, and 100 W provisioned for ConnectX-7, SSD and USB-C; and (forum 374791 #8) the 140 W figure "refers to the GB10 SoC TDP, not a guaranteed GPU-only power target". OEM specifications as printed in reviews: ASUS GX10 power input up to 180 W over USB PD 3.1 EPR from a 240 W adapter (StorageReview, PCMag); Dell ships a 280 W adapter (StorageReview); HP lists ~228 W peak and 36-38 W idle (StorageReview). The staff posts are forum statements, not documentation.
- [Community-measured + Interpretation] GPU Current Temp plus T.Limit sums to 94-97 °C across seven independent captures: 44+50, 52+45 (a second capture in the same post gives 52+44), 52+43 and 44+52 in forum posts, and own 58+38, 59+37 and 51+45. The manual defines T.Limit as the margin to the maximum operating temperature. SW thermal slowdown is reported at GPU temperatures of 83-89 °C, so the limit is probably evaluated on a hotter internal sensor than the one `nvidia-smi` reports. [Interpretation]
- [Community-measured] `nvidia-smi` GPU temperature reads 7-17 °C below the hottest ACPI thermal zone (griffith.mark, forum 372662 #43; ai-muninn: acpitz 92.2 °C vs GPU 84 °C one sample before a power-off; maci0: the board zone is "~10 C hotter than the GPU die"). The zones are `_TZ_.TSOC, TS0E, TS0P, TS1E, TS1P, TGPU, TUNC` (forum 378945 #13), and the SoC package zone (TSOC) runs hottest under load (AlexLJC).
- [Community-measured] Linux sees one thermal trip per ACPI zone: `critical` at 104 °C (tonyd2wild quotes 104.8 °C), with no cooling devices bound (tsarihan, forum 368536 #49; AlexLJC; tniccum). The OS does no thermal management below that. Fan control belongs to EC firmware, and there is no OS fan RPM or PWM path (maci0 `FAN_MONITORING.md`; NVIDIA moderator, forum 378945 #14).
- [Code-verified, vendor doc negative] NVIDIA's DGX Spark User Guide publishes a 140 W GB10 SoC TDP, a 240 W external supply, "100W is available for other system components", and an ideal ambient of 5-30 °C. The hardware, known-issues and release-notes pages publish no GPU clock, throttle temperature, shutdown temperature or fan behaviour (all fetched 2026-09-23).
- [Community-measured, NVIDIA moderator forum statements, not documentation] "Hitting an ACPI sensor value of ~95 °C and then rebooting means you're crossing a platform-level thermal safety limit, so the shutdown is protective" (forum 368536 #23). Later: "abrupt shut downs can be triggered by both thermal and current-related reasons", and the clock cap "reduces power transients that appear to trigger hard shutdowns at sustained loads around 75–90W" (forum 377478 #30).
- [Community-measured] At idle the EC sets fan speed from system power draw, not temperature. Idle units heat to 51-65 °C with the fans stopped, and any extra 4-5 W of load (a USB device, a cabled ConnectX-7) restores the fan (x1917x, forum 378945 #17-19, acknowledged by a moderator in #20). On uncabled single units the ConnectX-7 hot-plug driver powers the NIC off about 16 s after boot, which drops the box below that threshold (#22). A cabled ConnectX-7 raises idle draw and restores the fan on some units (x1917x, forum 380282: a link "almost doubles each unit's idle power"), but one cabled GX10 ring still had a unit idling 8-10 °C hotter, which its authors attribute to power-keyed EC fan control and support with a CPU-load test (NNNtrance hardware doc §1.2). Cabling is not a guaranteed fix. Two GX10s on matched kernel, driver, OTA, BIOS and EC/PD firmware diverged at idle (P8, 3.5-4.8 W) at 34-36 vs 55-58 °C GPU, and unplugging HDMI alone took the affected unit from 36 to 45 °C in five minutes; NVIDIA staff deferred to ASUS ("we do not have specifics on ASUS's cooling logic", forum 380282). Separately, a newer GX10 idled 8-12 °C hotter than its twin and ran ~10 °C hotter as a TP worker, throttling and sometimes shutting down. Its cabling at idle is not stated and the cause is unknown; heat under load is not explained by the idle-fan path, which points at a unit-level cooling fault (forum 371201 #36).
- [Community-measured + Interpretation] The GPU rail's share of wall power depends on the load, and none of the published ratios is a rule.
  - Idle: the GPU rail is "roughly 11 W of a 53 W idle draw at the wall" (agjs).
  - Compute-heavy single node: ~40-50%. Under llama.cpp prompt processing one GX10 drew 85-95 W on the rail against up to 180 W at the wall at stock, and 55 W against 125 W at 2100 (vasimv, forum 373199 #22). In a 45-minute llama.cpp run the rail was ~64 W against a "140 to 160 W" system figure whose meter is not named (Alex Ziskind video).
  - Light GPU load: ~21-27%. NVFP4 quantisation drew 16-20 W on the rail against 75-80 W on a Kill-A-Watt (HotHardware).
  - Two-node TP rack: `nvidia-smi` "only accounts for ~12-27% of the real draw" (peter.h177, forum 379389 #1).

  A GPU-rail percentage is never a system-power percentage.
- [Community-measured] GPU rail per workload class at stock clocks, each from the source's own tool:
  - single-stream decode, ~33-45 W: 32.6-44.3 W on a GX10 llama.cpp suite (pendakwahteknologi); ~35-38 W at 95% util on 26-27B models (forum 367768); "35-45W" for normal vLLM runs (forum 374791 #5); 44.8 W uncapped in nacyot's sweep;
  - batched decode, 35-46 W: StorageReview's decode-heavy phase (Dell 40-45 W, HP 35-46 W);
  - prefill-heavy peaks, 69-75.5 W: StorageReview's single-unit reviews and five-unit thermal test (ASUS 69.77, Dell ~70, HP 69, Founders Edition ~74, Gigabyte 75.54 W);
  - a sustained CUTLASS GEMM sweep: 67.7-68.4 W average, 76.9-81.4 W peak, settling at 62-65 °C (forum 367851);
  - burn tests: 93 W at 71 °C (forum 375986 #4, gpu-burn, one run) and 90.6-94.6 W on four healthy nodes in a 15 s fp16 burn (tonyd2wild latch doc);
  - under a heavier load, a multi-OEM test logged "SW power cap" at 99.12 W on all four units (Alex Ziskind video).

  Capped decode reads lower: 22 W at a 2000 cap (nacyot) and ~23 W at ~2,086 MHz (azampatti, forum 377375 #22).
- [Community-measured] Chassis alone moves temperature by 10-15 °C. StorageReview ran five OEM units side by side under the same prefill-heavy vLLM load at stock. GPU peak power was 69.3-76.0 W, GPU peak temperature 68 °C (Acer) vs 80-82 °C (the others), and CPU peak 74.6 °C vs 87-88 °C. Units of one model also differ: one owner's three units under the same stress test split, with one holding 2,450 MHz and two dipping below 2,200 MHz before shutting down, even with min=max locked at 2,450 (eugr, forum 363370 #36, #49). StorageReview's single-unit reviews, run under the same three-phase vLLM protocol, add per-component peaks: ASUS GX10 CPU 87.3 / GPU 82 / NVMe 56.8 / NIC 73 °C; Dell CPU ~87.7-88 / GPU ~80 °C (decode-heavy GPU steady at 68-71 °C); Gigabyte CPU 90 / GPU 81 °C; HP ZGX Nano G1n CPU 77.3 / GPU 69 °C. The HP review is a separate write-up, not part of the same side-by-side session. On identical llama.cpp runs a Gigabyte AI TOP ATOM drew 40-52 W against a GX10's 33-44 W (pendakwahteknologi/aitopatom-benchmarks @ a415ba72).
- [Community-measured] There is no cross-node clock coupling. The "Sync Boost" reason is a single-host multi-GPU feature, and every GB10 counter capture shows Sync Boost 0 µs (agjs, own, forum 370304). Each node of a tensor-parallel group runs at a clock set by its own thermal and power state, so ranks differ, and the pacing rank has to be measured rather than assumed. At matched GPU power, the head node's TSOC averaged 10.27 °C hotter than the worker's (AlexLJC). On a three-node EXL3 ring the head ran hottest (83.4 °C mean) but worker-2 held the lowest clock (2,356 vs 2,398 / 2,450 MHz) (NNNtrance). Own packets agree [Measured]: in the 2026-09-21 stock 250K arm, node-2 (a worker) accrued twice the head's new SW-thermal time and about four times its HW-thermal time; cumulative SW-thermal since the last boot was 28.3 min on node-2 against 16.8 min on node-1; and in a 2026-09-08 two-node spot check the head held the lower clock (2,405 vs 2,450 MHz at an equal 77 °C). Unthrottled binning spreads are small (2.5% in clock with identical memory bandwidth, NNNtrance), so for bandwidth-bound decode binning alone moves little. [Interpretation]
- [Community-measured] A rank can sit in a slow state that the usual telemetry does not show, and one slow rank paces the whole tensor-parallel group.
  - Latch. Two of four TP4 ranks (one Founders Edition, one GX10) sat at 631-949 MHz both idle and under load, while `nvidia-smi` showed P0, application clocks of 2,418 and no event reasons: "two slow GPUs set the pace for all four". Unplugging the adapter for 30-60 s cleared both (tonyd2wild `docs/gpu-clock-latch.md` @ d45538f). Other published signatures are 611 MHz at ~13-16 W with a misleading ~50 °C "cap" (Sggin1, GX10) and 721 MHz at ~10 W with `-lgc 3003` ignored (forum 376039). A GX10 held at 60 W / ~65 TFLOPS on one benchmark after several drains is an unresolved low-ceiling report, not an established latch (forum 374791).
  - Hidden slow state. On older stacks (Founders Edition BIOS 0ACUM018 with driver 580.142; GX10 on 580.159.03) one GPU flipped every 7-32 s between a fast state (decode-shaped GEMV 224-233 GB/s, fp16 matmul 76-84 TFLOPS) and a slow one (66-80 GB/s, 53-55 TFLOPS). In both states the SM clock read 2,164-2,177 MHz, the event reasons read 0x0, and the SW power-cap counter did not move at 100 ms sampling. Thermal causes were ruled out (zones 40-42 °C). In vLLM TP4 serving the two states meant ~63 vs ~94 ms per decode step. The state never appeared on eight Founders Edition nodes on BIOS 0ACUM027, driver 580.173.02 and kernel 6.17.0-1032, but the author's old-stack fleet also stopped showing it after a power cycle, so firmware generation is a suspect, not a finding (tonyd2wild issue #1).
- [Community-measured] A clock sample alone cannot tell idle from load. A GX10 read 2,411 MHz at ~10 W both at idle and while a model loaded, and 2,379-2,392 MHz under load (forum 371402, 5 s sampling). Another unit idled at 208 MHz, P8, 3.1 W (forum 375986 #5). Log power with every clock sample.
- [Community-measured] Deeper power telemetry exists only through unsupported code. The SoC firmware's System Power Budget Manager computes system, SoC-package, CPU-cluster, DC-input and GPU power, PL1/PL2/SysPL1/SysPL2 limit averages, energy accumulators and PROCHOT, and "the firmware uses a 100 ms PID control loop that causes instantaneous values to oscillate". Only a community kernel driver reads it; its author calls it "vibe coded", and its CPU channels depend on firmware version (antheas/spark_hwmon @ 352c76e5). An energy-observability audit of the GX10 found "no CPU energy counter, no INA power-rail monitor, no IPMI/BMC, and no SCMI powercap protocol through any supported software interface" (arXiv:2605.27599v3, abstract). A firmware loop at 100 ms means that even a 100 ms NVML poll can miss clamps. [Interpretation]
- [Code-verified, vendor doc] The NVIDIA support page declares noise per ECMA-109 in the operating mode "max GPU stress in 25°C ambient": LWA,m 35 dB and LpA,m 29 dB (19 and 13 dB at idle). The compliance page says "it is normal for the system and/or AC power adapter to get warm" and repeats the 5-30 °C operating range. Neither page publishes a throttle or shutdown temperature.
- [Code-verified, vendor doc] Nsight Compute's aarch64 SBSA clock-control exclusion names "GA10b (Orin), GB10b (Thor)", not GB10, so `ncu` clock control applies on GB10 by default. `--clock-control` takes `base` (rated-TDP clocks), `boost` (the default; falls back to `base` if unsupported), `force-boost` (no fallback), `none`, and `reset` (clears a lock left by a killed `ncu` run and exits). Nsight Compute 2025.4 moved the default lock from base to boost on Ampere and newer. What `boost` resolves to on GB10 is untested (§8).

## 5. Levers, ranked

1. **Uniform static GPU clock cap on every rank, 2,100-2,200 MHz** (`nvidia-smi -lgc 0,<cap>`). This lever is the wiki's single statement on capping: cap for heat and throttling, not speed. Expect 3-6% on cold prefill; single-stream decode was 5-6% lower by mean, inside spread, on the own TP3 lane. Qualify per lane with 13's A/B/A rule before keeping the cap. Applies to every engine (it is host-level), every model family and TP1/TP2/TP3. The evidence is strongest on vLLM MoE lanes, with one community llama.cpp sweep; SGLang and ds4 are unmeasured.
   - Heat and power gain: peak GPU −7 to −12 °C. Own sweep −7 / −10 °C at 2200 / 2100; agjs soak −8 / −12 °C; MiaAI −9 °C. GPU rail −27% to −36% in prefill (own) and −36% in a soak (agjs). Throttle time goes to zero in every controlled cap arm: own sweep, agjs soak, and a throttling Founders Edition pair. [Measured] [Community-measured] The one two-node wall-meter sweep saw −17% at 2200 (330 -> 274 W, load pattern unstated). On llama.cpp decode, GPU package power fell 38% at 2200 and 50% at 2000 (nacyot), and one single-node wall reading fell 31% at 2100 under llama.cpp prompt processing (vasimv, no throughput given). [Community-measured]
   - Prefill and compute cost: −3.0% at 2200 and −4.9% at 2100 on a 250K cold prefill (own); −4.2% (MiaAI, 100K) and +3.9% time (agjs, 35K) at 2200; about 1.4-2.6% per 100 MHz below ~2.4 GHz. [Interpretation, recomputed across agjs, MiaAI, own and peter.h177] Diffusion and video jobs take +1% to +13% longer (nacyot's video arm: +1.1% at 2200, +6.9% at 2000). The mechanism shows at kernel level: on one node a compute-bound matmul lost ~10% at 2200 while a bandwidth-bound GEMV lost nothing (im0xMagnus). [Community-measured]
   - Decode cost depends on conditions and is not zero. It is inside ±2% for controlled bandwidth-bound single-stream runs: agjs −1.0% (n=20, not significant), MiaAI −0.7%, KojiChou within noise. It rises with concurrency: unlocking gives +2.3% at N=8 against −1.2% at N=1 (single observations). Some single-node runs lose more (rafaelkallis −6.6% at c1; azampatti ~10% on one A3B NVFP4 model, protocol unstated), and so did the own TP2 result, −6.8% (n=3, confounded by a firmware change between runs). The own TP3 sweep's C1 means were 4.9-6.4% lower at 1900-2200 with first requests excluded, inside the rep spread (n=5), the same size as the TP2 result. The first llama.cpp sweep sits inside the same band: −2.4% at 2200 and −4.6% at 2000 (nacyot, single node, median of 3). When stock is already throttling (one defective pair, later RMA'd), the effect depended on context depth: −21% at 32K and ×2.3 at 262K (tniccum). That is one report, not a general depth rule. [Community-measured] [Measured]
   - Risk: a cap can mask a defective unit, since NVIDIA staff describe power-offs that "only show up on specific units". It is not always enough: one unit at 2200 "still throttled in some situations even down to 1900Mhz", and a pair shut down at 2000 during a 200K prefill. It does not reliably cool CPU zones (lever 6), and it does not fix a latched or slow rank (lever 2).
   - When not to cap: if the workload "breathes" (idle gaps between bursts), the cap buys little (ai-muninn turned it off again for that reason). [Community-measured]
   - Smallest falsifying experiment: on the target lane, run an interleaved A B A B at stock vs cap. Use n≥20 single-stream decode per arm, n≥5 nonce-cold prefills at the lane's real mean prompt size, and a ≥8 min soak per arm with counters diffed per rank. The cap fails if the TTFT-weighted cost exceeds 3%, or if peak temperature drops less than 5 °C, or if throttle seconds per load second do not drop (agjs decision rule).
2. **Per-rank health check before any benchmark or tensor-parallel launch; clear a latched rank.** Use it also when decode varies boot to boot (see [Profiling protocol](14-profiling-protocol.md) lever 11). Applies to every engine and topology, and matters most on TP groups, where one slow rank paces all of them. Gain: large when a rank is bad, zero when every rank is healthy. A latched rank runs at 631-949 MHz against ~2.2-2.3 GHz healthy, and two latched ranks set the pace for a four-rank group (tonyd2wild latch doc). A hidden slow state cost ~63 vs ~94 ms per decode step (tonyd2wild issue #1). [Community-measured] Checks: a ≥30 s GEMM preflight (healthy is a mean clock ≥1,400 MHz and ≥40 W, Sggin1; own records use the same floor [Historical diagnostic]), a 15 s fp16 burn (healthy ≥80 W and 75-90 TFLOPS at ~2.2-2.4 GHz), and a memory-bound GEMV probe, because the hidden state shows only in bandwidth and power. Tools: parallelArchitect's NVML fork decodes the reason bitmask, adds a 100 ms timeline and reports INCONCLUSIVE below 80% utilisation; its "zero mask plus low clock means bad USB PD negotiation" verdict is the tool's heuristic, not a proof [Code-verified]. Cost: under a minute per rank. Clearing a latch needs a cold drain (adapter unplugged 30-60 s), which is a power cycle and needs the operator's explicit per-node approval. Risk: a drain does not clear every unit (§7). Smallest falsifying experiment: run the three checks on every rank before each TP benchmark for ten consecutive boots; if every rank passes every time, the lever has no value on that fleet beyond insurance.
3. **Cap level: pick the point on the prefill-vs-heat curve.** Applies to any lane already capped. Own sweep at 250K cold prefill: 2200 costs −3.0% at 81 °C peak and 47 W; 2100 costs −4.9% at 78 °C and 42 W; 1900 costs −9.1% at 73 °C and 33 W. On a 250K prompt that is about +5 / +8 / +16 s of TTFT. [Measured] The own fleet chose 2100 over 2200 for a 3 °C and 5 W margin at +3 s per 250K prompt. [Measured] Energy per token may bottom out lower, around 1,700-1,900 MHz (1,350-1,381 Wh per 1M tokens against 1,688 uncapped), but that sweep's request mix is unstated. [Community-measured, text only] NVIDIA staff relay 2100 as "reported as stable by multiple users" and point to 1400 MHz for the smallest transients. Community picks without controlled tables: nacyot recommends 2000 for llama.cpp decode (−4.6% at −50% GPU power), azampatti names 2000 and says "never run above 2100" (forum 377375 #19), and neko-legends and the MiaAI-lineage recipes use 2200. [Community-measured] Cost: prefill and compute time rise monotonically as the clock drops. Risk: below ~2,000 MHz, compute-bound jobs pay near-linearly (g6.67300: −9.0% at 1800). Smallest falsifying experiment: two adjacent caps in one boot on the same lane, a ≥8 min soak each, 250K cold prefill ×3 each, then compare peak hottest-zone temperature and TTFT.
4. **Airflow, placement and chassis.** Applies to all engines and topologies. Gain: unstacking gave −20 °C at idle [Measured, 2026-08-16 bake-off]. A reversed chassis fan fixed gave 94-96 -> 77 °C on the board zone (maci0). Cooling designs differ by 10-15 °C GPU and CPU under the same load (StorageReview). Dust cleaning resolved one shutdown case (forum 383821 #1). [Community-measured] More airflow results, each a single report and each a confounder for any cap A/B, so never change airflow between arms [Community-measured]:
   - Removing the cowling on a Gigabyte AI TOP ATOM and an MSI EdgeXpert gave a ~16 °C average drop; one summary gives 80 -> 64 °C peak at 2,444 MHz, unit not named, c=10 at 4K depth (forum 370656 #1).
   - The GX10's front grille is cosmetic: air enters at the bottom and leaves at the rear (forum 370656 #7; 367883 #8). A 120 mm fan blowing into the bottom vents took heat-soaked temperatures from 82 to 72-74 °C at stock clocks (danielbitpro, quoted in the 0xWhiteMage X thread).
   - Two USB 40 mm push fans gave ~10 °C (forum 367883 #1); a metal riser gave −6 °C on runs under two hours and −4 °C on longer ones (#17); a DIY push-fan setup similar to the 40 mm one gave 2-3 °C (#4).
   - In a ducted-cage thread, 2,000 RPM on the external fan did not clear throttling, and frontal airflow made an ASUS node worse while a single fan at 4,000 RPM helped it (forum 365302).

   Cost: close to none. Risk: none known. Smallest falsifying experiment: the same soak at a fixed cap before and after the physical change, logging the hottest ACPI zone per node.
5. **Cool-before-prefill gate.** Before any 200K+ cold prefill, wait until the hottest `/sys/class/thermal/thermal_zone*/temp` reads below 78 °C (poll every 30 s). Applies to deep-context single-node and TP lanes, most of all at stock clocks. Gain: it prevented a repeat of a 95 °C CPU-zone excursion during paced 300K/450K probes [Measured, operational, 2026-08-16 bake-off]. There is no throughput A/B. Cost: queueing delay on back-to-back deep prompts. Risk: it only gates the start, because a running prefill cannot be cancelled promptly in vLLM (§2). Smallest falsifying experiment: ten back-to-back 250K cold prefills with and without the gate at stock, counting throttle seconds and the peak hottest-zone temperature.
6. **CPU-side heat on the tensor-parallel head node.** The lever is a CPU `scaling_max_freq` cap, the vLLM `SpinCondition` bounded sleep, or SGLang `--sleep-on-idle`. Applies to vLLM TP head nodes (spin) and any node where the SoC or CPU zones run hotter than the GPU. The evidence is contested:
   - A CPU cap gave 92 -> 84 °C with identical wall time under vLLM TP busy-polling (tniccum). It gave no change under all-core CPU stress (tsarihan, 92 °C at every CPU clock). One pair needed it, together with `-lgc 200,1800`, to survive a 200K prefill (nvidia3978). Capping only the performance cores (3.9 -> 2.8 GHz) cut the SoC zone 9 °C under vLLM TP=2 load, with single-stream speed unchanged and 8 streams −5% (nacyot).
   - The bounded sleep cut the head-minus-worker gap by 2.85 °C and cut time at ≥95 °C from 8.25% to 1.02%, with no throughput measurement (AlexLJC). A second fix to the same spin (1 s -> 2 ms) cut vLLM CPU from 333% to 89% and the SoC zone by 11 °C, but a later re-verification found single-stream decode 2-6% slower, inside run-to-run variance (nacyot). [Community-measured]
   - SGLang `--sleep-on-idle` is unmeasured. [Proposed]
   - Cost: for the spin patch, up to 2-6% single-stream decode in one re-verification and not established in the other, plus a code change to carry; the CPU cap cost 5% at 8 streams in one report and may slow other CPU-bound work. Risk: the result depends on the workload. Smallest falsifying experiment: a matched soak on a TP2 lane with TSOC logged per rank, with and without the change, plus decode n≥20 per arm.
7. **Firmware choice and fan verification.** Applies to every node after any EC or firmware update. Gain: the own fleet stayed on a firmware branch without a reported EC fan-controller regression; after the flash, fans responded and temperatures read 46-55 °C [Measured]. Community reports tie hot zones and fans that do not ramp to EC/UEFI updates (forum 377044), and an ASUS EC curve reverse-engineered from firmware reaches 100% only at 97 °C (48% at 85 °C). The FE EC fan targets reverse-engineered from firmware are 54% at 90 °C and 100% at 101 °C (profile A) [Community-measured]. Firmware generation is also the leading suspect for the hidden slow state, which appeared only on older BIOS and driver stacks (§3), though the link is untested. An EC rollback as a fan-curve fix is contested, and the weight of evidence is against it (§7). [Community-measured] Cost: a flash window. Risk: an EC change moves fan and throttle behaviour silently. Smallest falsifying experiment: the same soak before and after a flash, logging hottest-zone temperature and the counters.
8. **Volatile EC fan override.** Applies to single nodes with the community EC-mailbox tool. Gain: −4.5 °C on the hottest zone at 9,000 RPM (forum 380995 #11). [Community-measured] Cost: noise. Risk: unsupported and firmware-specific, and it does not survive a reset; untested on a TP group. Smallest falsifying experiment: the same soak at a fixed cap with and without the override, counters diffed.
9. **Closed-loop clock governor.** A script reads the hottest ACPI zone every 3-8 s and steps the clock (maci0: 150 MHz steps between 1,500 and 3,003 MHz with 87/91 °C hysteresis and a 94 °C panic drop; griffith.mark: target 80 °C, 8 s loop). pju-hoge runs a PID controller on the hottest ACPI zone at a setpoint of 80 °C with a 3 s poll: under llama.cpp it walked the cap from 3,003 to 2,224 MHz within 18 s at ~88-90 W, held 2,102-2,224 MHz at 56-62 W with the board at 79-81 °C for the ~19-minute logged run (the README labels it hour-long; its timestamps do not support that), returned to 3,003 MHz within ~6 s of load dropping, and calls `-rgc` on clean exit. [Community-measured] Applies to single nodes. On a TP group every rank would need identical steps (lever 1), which none of these tools does. Gain: no published throughput A/B against a static cap. [Proposed] Cost: a daemon to maintain. Risk: the rank-to-rank clock variance that a uniform cap removes, and a loop 1-3 orders of magnitude slower than a power transient. Smallest falsifying experiment: a single-node soak, governor vs static 2100, comparing TTFT, decode and the counters.
10. **Engine duty cycle (ds4 `--power N`).** Applies to ds4 and the Entrpi fork only, and the README says V4.1 and GLM currently require `--power 100`. Gain: unmeasured on GB10. [Proposed] Cost: throughput falls in proportion on prefill and decode alike, by construction [Code-verified]. It needs no root. Risk: it cuts decode, which a clock cap mostly spares. Smallest falsifying experiment: single-node DeepSeek V4 Flash at `--power 100` vs `80`, decode n≥20 and a ≥8 min soak, with GPU rail and counters.

## 6. Protocol

This is the procedure an agent follows to A/B test a clock change, to make a chosen cap persistent, and to lock clocks for profiling.

**Preconditions.**
- Fleet rules from the entry page ([GB10 Inference Wiki](README.md)):
  - no memory overcommit;
  - one variable per boot;
  - stop if `MemAvailable` falls under 4 GiB on any node;
  - a clean window with no outside traffic (poll the running-request count every second);
  - if an A/B needs an engine restart, skip the image pull;
  - never restart an agent gateway;
  - never `pkill -f` a pattern that matches your own shell.
- A clock change is a live host setting and needs no engine restart. Run every arm inside one engine boot so that the clock is the only variable. [Measured, the 2026-09-21 sweep did this]
- Record the identity first: driver, kernel, EC and SoC firmware, chassis model and placement (stacked or not), and ambient if known.
- Check for an existing lock. Run `systemctl list-unit-files | grep -i clock` and read `clocks.current.sm` under a short load on every rank. Do not use "Applications Clocks" or "Max Clocks", which never change. [Community-measured, MiaAI-Lab hidden unit; ai-muninn]
- Check root: `sudo -n nvidia-smi` works on every rank, and `-lgc 0,<cap>` followed by `-rgc` changes the reported loaded clock. [Community-measured, agjs preflight]
- Read the cumulative counters (`nvidia-smi -q -d PERFORMANCE`: SW power capping, SW thermal, HW thermal, HW power brake, sync boost) on every rank, and read the hottest `/sys/class/thermal/thermal_zone*/temp` per rank.
- Health-check every rank before the first arm (§5 lever 2): a ≥30 s GEMM preflight (mean clock ≥1,400 MHz and ≥40 W), a short fp16 burn (≥80 W and 75-90 TFLOPS at ~2.2-2.4 GHz; a latched rank reads ~700-950 MHz and <20 W), and a memory-bound GEMV probe. A failing rank voids the run; clearing a latch needs a cold drain, which is a power cycle and needs the operator's explicit per-node approval. [Community-measured, tonyd2wild latch doc and issue #1; Sggin1]
- Identify the pacing rank from clocks under load and counter deltas; do not assume it is the head. [Community-measured, NNNtrance] [Measured, own 2026-09-21 and 2026-09-08 packets]
- Record airflow and fan aids, chassis placement, and every other resident container or model, and change none of them between arms. [Community-measured, §5 lever 4; forum 348760 #64]

**The change.**
- Apply `sudo nvidia-smi -lgc 0,<cap>` on every rank with the same value, before the arm starts. A head-only cap "measures nothing", because TP ranks run in lockstep. [Community-measured, peter.h177; MiaAI-Lab harness lines 13-15] Leave `--mode` at its default; `--mode=1` has no GB10 report.
- Verify the lock with `clocks.sm` under load together with power, never with a reason bit or the applications clock: the lock takes effect while `applications_clocks_setting` reads "Not Active", and on driver 595 the applications-clock query returns a string. [Community-measured, im0xMagnus; nacyot]
- Wait at least 3 s (agjs `set_clock`), then send one warm-up request and discard it, since the first request after a new lock read slow three times in the own sweep. [Measured] Do the same after any idle gap longer than about a minute: an external TP4 fleet ran its first request after ~13 min idle at 94 vs 63 ms/step, while 45 s of idle did not trigger it. [Community-measured, tonyd2wild issue #1]

**Arm design.**
- Keep every arm inside one engine boot and one host boot. Identical configurations have differed by 11% across boots (bimodal ~65 vs ~74 tok/s, neko-legends) and by 15.9% in C8 (NNNtrance), so any comparison across boots needs an A/B/A plus a fresh per-rank health check. [Community-measured]
- Interleave A B A B, or use A/B/A with a drift gate: if a1 and a2 differ by more than 1%, the run is unstable. For a sweep, measure stock at both ends and discard the run if they differ by more than the decode standard error. [Community-measured, agjs `AGENTS.md` lines 53-55; MiaAI-Lab harness lines 159-164]
- Cool every arm to the same start temperature (the own sweep started each arm at 51-55 °C). Never compare a warm host with a cold one.
- Decode: n≥20 per arm, since per-rep SD is about 4 tok/s and a gap under ~2.5 tok/s is noise. Pre-register the decode threshold and rep count before the run. Two own A/Bs reached opposite verdicts on thresholds chosen afterwards. [Community-measured] [Measured]
- Prefill: nonce at token 0; assert `cached_tokens == 0`; count tokens from the usage block; n≥5 at the lane's real mean prompt size (`vllm:request_prompt_tokens_sum / _count`), plus one deep prompt (≥200K) if the lane serves them.
- Temperature: a ≥8 min soak per arm. A request/response benchmark "reliably records zero throttling on hardware that `soak.sh` drives to 90 C minutes later". [Community-measured, agjs `AGENTS.md` line 47] TensorRT's measurement guidance makes the related point for power throttling: idle gaps between inferences raise the average clock and overstate throughput, so its benchmark tool leaves nearly no gaps, and NVIDIA gives "no definite recommendation on whether the clock should be locked". [Code-verified, vendor doc; applying it to thermal soaks is Interpretation]
- Weight the verdict by the server's own prefill share, `time_to_first_token_seconds_sum / e2e_request_latency_seconds_sum`.

**Record (packet fields).**
- Lock value and lock state per rank.
- Median `clocks.current.sm` under load per rank per arm, with GPU power logged in the same sample (a clock sample alone cannot tell idle from load).
- Per-rank health-check results, the pacing rank, and the boot identifier.
- Arm start temperature, peak and end GPU temperature, and the hottest ACPI zone, per rank.
- GPU rail W, mean and max per rank, labelled "GPU rail (nvidia-smi)". Wall W labelled "wall meter" if metered.
- Counter deltas (pre/post each arm, per rank, normalised to load seconds, never to uptime).
- Sampler cadence.
- Per-rep values, not only means, with the first request after a lock change or an idle gap flagged.
- A per-request tok/s timeline. In one log-less power-off, generation throughput collapsed 47 -> 31 -> 20 -> 0 tok/s over the final ~40 s, which may be an earlier signal than any clock or temperature sample. [Interpretation, one report, forum 377069 #7]
- Engine, image and recipe.
- Firmware and EC version.
- The fleet cap state, in every later packet's identity block. Any cross-date comparison that straddles a clock change needs a same-day control or a labelled adjustment. [Measured, 2026-09-23 V3.2 cross-date confound]

**Pass gate** (agjs decision rule, adopted). Apply the cap only if all three hold:
- the TTFT-weighted cost is at or under 3%;
- throttle seconds per load second drop;
- peak temperature drops by at least 5 °C.

Also confirm that the median loaded clock sits within one snap step of the target on every rank. If the thermal gain is nil, revert even at zero cost. [Community-measured, agjs `AGENTS.md` lines 80-86]

**Persist.**
- Install a oneshot unit: `Type=oneshot`, `RemainAfterExit=yes`, `After=`/`Wants=nvidia-persistenced.service`, `ExecStart=/usr/bin/nvidia-smi -lgc 0,<cap>`, `ExecStop=/usr/bin/nvidia-smi -rgc`, `WantedBy=multi-user.target`. Four of five community units carry the `ExecStop`, so `systemctl stop` restores stock. [Community-measured]
- Persistence mode does not carry a lock across a reboot. [Community-measured; vendor doc: `-pm` "does not persist across reboots"] NVML states it directly: "After system reboot or driver reload GPU clocks go back to their default value". GB10 users confirm that reboots and fleet power cycles clear the lock (tonyd2wild issue #1; forum 378300). [Code-verified, vendor doc; Community-measured]
- The persistence daemon keeps GPU state loaded while it holds the device file open, so state survives between CUDA jobs. Its documentation says nothing about clocks; the reading that a lock lasts for the life of the loaded driver and no longer is [Interpretation]. Most published GB10 units order themselves after `nvidia-persistenced`; one orders after `multi-user.target` and carries `ExecStop=... -rgc`. [Community-measured]
- Add a device wait: an `ExecStartPre` that polls for `/dev/nvidia0` (for example 30 × 2 s) and fails if the device never appears, before `ExecStart` (neko-legends unit). It addresses boot order without testing it. A governor unit should call `-rgc` on clean exit so that a crashed loop cannot leave the GPU stuck at a low clock (pju-hoge). [Code-verified at the pinned SHAs]
- Stop GPU containers before `systemctl daemon-reload`. With the systemd cgroup driver and the legacy injection path, a reload can revoke GPU access from running containers ("Failed to initialize NVML: Unknown Error"). [Code-verified, vendor doc: NVIDIA Container Toolkit troubleshooting; agjs `install_cap.sh` lines 5-7]
- "Enabled and active is not proof": after an approved reboot of one node, re-check the loaded clock before calling the cap persistent. Reboots need the operator's explicit per-node approval.

**Clock-locked profiling.**
- Lock min=max externally (e.g. `sudo nvidia-smi -lgc 2100,2100`) on every rank and pass `ncu --clock-control none`. By default `ncu` "attempts to limit GPU clock frequencies to their boost value", and "thermal throttling directed by the hardware or driver ... always overrides". [Code-verified, vendor doc: Nsight Compute Profiling Guide §2.6.2]
- Nsight Systems does not lock clocks.
- After a killed `ncu` run, `--clock-control reset` clears the lock that `ncu` left. Whether it also clears an external `-lgc` cap is untested, so re-read `clocks.sm` under load after any killed profile. [Code-verified, vendor doc: Nsight Compute CLI; the external-cap effect is Interpretation]
- CUPTI recommends `nvidia-smi --lock-gpu-clocks=tdp,tdp` for consistent multi-pass metrics (in its MIG subsection). NVML documents symbolic pairs (`tdp,tdp`, `unlimited,tdp`, `tdp,unlimited`, `unlimited,unlimited`) "for Volta or newer fully supported devices", and mixing numeric and symbolic values returns INVALID_ARGUMENT. None of this is tested on GB10. [Code-verified, vendor doc]
- A min=max lock can still dip under thermal or power limiting (eugr: units locked at 2,450 dipped below 2,200), so log the clock during the capture.
- Profiles locate a bottleneck; the unprofiled A/B above decides whether a fix worked ([Profiling protocol](14-profiling-protocol.md)).

**Rollback.**
- Live: `sudo nvidia-smi -rgc` on every rank reverts at once, with no engine restart. A reboot or a driver reload also clears a lock that no unit re-applies (NVML).
- Persistent: `sudo systemctl disable --now <unit>`. Without an `ExecStop`, follow it with an explicit `-rgc`.
- Verify: the median loaded clock returns to ~2.40 GHz on every rank.

**Acceptance on real traffic** (owed on the own fleet). Compare one day of real agent traffic at the cap with a stock day, using per-request TTFT and decode metrics plus dashboard temperatures and counter deltas. [Proposed]

## 7. Anti-patterns and known-bad

- [Community-measured] Calling 3,003 MHz "stock" or "default". It is a readout; the loaded stock clock is ~2.40-2.46 GHz. One published write-up calls 3,003 MHz the default and describes GB10 memory as HBM. Its temperature result (78-88 -> 65-69 °C at 2200) is usable, but its mechanism text is not (Wild Pines, fetched 2026-09-23).
- [Community-measured] Verifying a lock from "Applications Clocks" or "Max Clocks". They never change; read the current clock under load.
- [Community-measured] `nvidia-smi -pl` as a lever. Every power limit is N/A on GB10. A widely cited `-pl 320` result came from a different GPU (forum 383624 #3).
- [Community-measured] Raising the floor to raise the clock. `-lgc 2418,3003` left the loaded clock at 2,463-2,496 MHz. A lock is a ceiling or target, and thermal or power limiting overrides it.
- [Community-measured] Believing persistence mode carries a lock across a reboot. It does not (NVIDIA moderator, forum 377478 #30; agjs; forum 372662 #17 correcting #8).
- [Historical diagnostic] A non-persistent lock drifting silently. An ad-hoc lock on one own node disappeared and reappeared across sessions and reboots, so the benchmarks in between ran under an unrecorded clock state. Persist the cap and log clocks in every packet.
- [Community-measured] A forgotten persistent lock contaminating published numbers. A public two-node recipe measured all its numbers capped for about 2.5 weeks (MiaAI-Lab commit 4bac6e26).
- [Measured] Comparing across dates when a clock change sits in between. A capped-vs-pre-cap prefill gap of ~8% was mostly the cap and needed a labelled −5.7% adjustment (2026-09-23 V3.2 verification).
- [Measured] Reading decode A/Bs with thresholds chosen after the fact. The own TP2 A/B rejected a cap on −6.8% (n=3). The later TP3 sweep accepted one with −5.8% of means inside the rep spread. A controlled community TP2 run on the same model family found −1.0% at n=20. Pre-register the threshold and the reps.
- [Measured] Judging thermals from a request/response micro-benchmark. The own 2026-08-16 A/B read "temperature ~nil" under a cap, while soaks show −8 to −12 °C (agjs), −9 °C (MiaAI) and −10 °C (own sweep). agjs: `ab_test.sh` "reliably records zero throttling on hardware that `soak.sh` drives to 90 C minutes later".
- [Measured] Quoting an operational estimate as a cost. The 2026-08-16 bake-off's "prefill pays ~10-20% at 2200" is superseded by controlled −3.0% to −5.5% at 2200 (own sweep, agjs, MiaAI).
- [Community-measured] "Decode is free under a cap" as a universal rule. It holds for controlled bandwidth-bound single-stream decode, and a kernel-level probe shows why (GEMV 0% at 2200, matmul −10%). It fails at higher concurrency, on some single-node MoE runs, and when stock is already throttling: one defective pair lost 21% at 32K depth and gained ×2.3 at 262K (§4, §5 lever 1).
- [Community-measured] "Capping both ranks raises sustained throughput by 29%". It did not reproduce: −0.7% decode, −4.2% prefill, and 0 µs thermal slowdown in either state (MiaAI-Lab commit 4bac6e26).
- [Community-measured] "2200 MHz eliminates throttling". This depends on the unit: one unit still throttled at 1900-2200 but not at 2000 (forum 372662 #35), a pair shut down at 2000 (forum 368536 #38), and on a throttling pair 2200 "re-engaged mild throttling" on the hotter unit (forum 380238 #1).
- [Community-measured] Assuming the head node paces a tensor-parallel group. On a three-node EXL3 ring the head ran hottest but worker-2 held the lowest clock (NNNtrance), and in the own 2026-09-21 stock arm the worker accrued more thermal-slowdown time than the head [Measured]. Measure the pacing rank per boot.
- [Community-measured] Assuming a cabled ConnectX-7 fixes idle heating. It raises idle draw and restores the fan on some units, but one cabled GX10 ring still had a unit idling 8-10 °C hotter (NNNtrance). This supersedes the first version of this page (2026-09-23), which said cabled cluster nodes are not affected; that was an inference from the hot-plug mechanism, not a measurement.
- [Community-measured] Quoting "15.9% of compute" or "leaving one node capped costs all three 16%" as a cap cost. The source gives no cap value, protocol or per-arm data, and its own CHANGELOG uses 15.9% for a boot-to-boot C8 spread with nothing changed (NNNtrance).
- [Community-measured] Reading "cold 32K prefill ~950 -> ~2100 tok/s" as the effect of a 2200 lock. The README says "(was ~950 pre-cleanup)", so the jump spans other changes (neko-legends).
- [Community-measured] EC firmware rollback as a fan-curve fix. The original post was written by an agent and claims 35-37 °C at 120-125 W per node after rolling back, which no other load reading supports. NVIDIA staff could not reproduce the problem on a Founders Edition and sent OEM owners to their vendor. On a 2× Founders Edition cluster the rollback changed nothing and only `-lgc 200,2100` helped. PowerStress still failed at 97.4 °C after a rollback. One owner found it helped, and tied the lockups to near-OOM ("50/50 of OOM or thermal lockup"). The weight of evidence is against it (forum 377069).
- [Community-measured] Assuming a cold drain always clears a low-clock latch. It usually does (tonyd2wild latch doc, Sggin1, forum 376039 #6), but one 721 MHz unit did not clear after an unplug of up to 8 h (forum 376039 #4). Two theories compete, and neither is proven: the adapter overheating or entering protection, so that the drain resets the brick rather than the unit (forum 376039 #5, #7; an owner in a 30-35 °C room suspects adapter heat, forum 373199 #8), and PD-controller firmware (Sggin1; nacyot). A 60 W / ~65 TFLOPS ceiling on one GX10 is an unresolved report, not an established latch; staff said the 140 W figure "may overlap with a low-power or degraded-performance state" and asked for data (forum 374791 #8).
- [Interpretation] Reading the SGLang idle-resident-models result as a mechanism. Stopping three idle containers took the SoC zone from the 90s to 83 °C and doubled throughput, but it may also have removed their CPU, memory-bandwidth or polling load, and the arms ran for very different lengths without a fan or ambient log (forum 348760 #64). Treat it as a hypothesis.
- [Community-measured] Using a whole-setup wall reading as a node figure. A "240 W idle, 380-420 W sustained, 510 W peak" reading for "the GB10" exceeds a single node's 240 W adapter and every single-node wall reading in §4 (Talki blog). Unusable as a node figure.
- [Community-measured] Quoting clearances as NVIDIA's. A third-party setup guide gives "10 cm front, 2 cm sides, 40 cm rear" "per NVIDIA Quick Start Guide", plus "~233 W" and "77-80 °C GPU / ~85 °C CPU at ~18 °C ambient" (Enverge). No NVIDIA page fetched contains them, and the Quick Start Guide PDF was not located, so they are unverified, not disproved.
- [Code-verified, vendor doc] Applying Grace-platform power capping to GB10. The `-pl <W> -sc 0|1` scopes, Module Power readings and automatic power steering are documented for Grace Hopper and GB200; on GB10 every power limit reads N/A. The community SPBM caps exist but are unsupported, and no GB10 A/B uses them (antheas/spark_hwmon).
- [Community-measured] "There is no way to see CPU power" and "a community driver reads CPU power" are both true. NVIDIA staff describe supported interfaces (forum 360631); the community driver reads firmware shared memory without support, and an audit paper confirms that the firmware computes per-rail energy but does not expose it (arXiv:2605.27599v3). Staff never reconciled the two.
- [Community-measured] Comparing `clocks.applications.graphics` as a number in a health check. On driver 595 it returns "Requested functionality has been deprecated" (nacyot). Read `clocks.sm`.
- [Community-measured] Telling idle from load by clock alone. A GX10 read ~2,400 MHz both at ~10 W idle and at 67-83 W under load (forum 371402). Log power with every clock sample.
- [Historical diagnostic] "A documented ~95 °C shutdown". No NVIDIA document states a shutdown temperature; an own packet wrongly called it documented. The community band is 92-98 °C on the hottest ACPI zone. There is one moderator forum statement ("~95 °C ACPI ... platform-level thermal safety limit"), and ai-muninn states that its data "does not support a threshold".
- [Community-measured] Normalising throttle counters to uptime. "On an idle-heavy host the uptime denominator understates throttling by roughly 100x." Normalise to load seconds.
- [Interpretation] Reading a large SW-power-capping counter as a fault. On a single-node compute-heavy box it accumulates from many short clamps; with good cooling it dominates the thermal counters by orders of magnitude (AlexLJC 2,063x; tsarihan: "the clocks sit where the power cap puts them"). The individual clamp duration is unmeasured (§8).
- [Measured] Trusting a single live snapshot. An own snapshot at an 87 °C peak showed no active reason while the lifetime SW-thermal counter was nonzero. The event-reason block is instantaneous and the counters are cumulative, so diff the counters across the window.
- [Community-measured] Trusting a coarse sampler to see a clamp or a power-off. Published cadences:
  - 1 Hz (agjs, MiaAI-Lab, StorageReview, forum 377365) and 1 s off-box over UDP (forum 383624 #9);
  - 2 s (ai-muninn, tonyd2wild) and 3 s (maci0 governor; forum 377044 #12);
  - 10 s (Wild Pines; forum 383624 #1) and 30 s (dev.to governor);
  - 1 min (forum 373251, which caught only two loaded samples before power loss).

  An NVML fork with a 100 ms timeline is mentioned in forum 371201, and parallelArchitect/spark-gpu-throttle-check publishes one. In forum 383624 the last 10 s sample before a freeze read "81 °C, 60 W, no throttle bits". Even 100 ms is not always enough: a 100 ms `nvidia-smi` poll missed a 3.3× bandwidth state flip while the counters did not move (tonyd2wild issue #1), the firmware power loop itself runs at 100 ms (antheas/spark_hwmon), and 1 s logging missed a 96 -> 76 W clamp ("maybe because it happens between the seconds", Alex Ziskind video). No source has measured individual clamp durations. Diff the cumulative counters across the window instead, and log power next to clocks.
- [Community-measured] Using `utilization.gpu` as evidence of work. A deadlocked vLLM engine read ~95% util at 16-27 W against ~45 W healthy (forum 368536 #36). Power is the discriminator.
- [Community-measured] Misreading a low-power wedge as throttling. Signatures under load include:
  - ~630-650 MHz at 15-18 W with clean event reasons (forum 371201); a community diagnosis blames an EC reduced-power envelope after a USB-PD event, and a full cold power cycle (cable out ≥30 s) restored ~52 W;
  - 513 MHz with every reason "Not Active" and zero counters (forum 361296);
  - 650 MHz at 15 W (forum 370304), where the moderator advised unplugging the unit and the power supply from the wall.

  Own [Historical diagnostic]: ~611 MHz at ~13 W is recorded as a firmware wedge, and a healthy loaded floor is ≥1,400 MHz and ≥40 W. Detectors: hoesing flags <1,400 MHz under a cuBLAS load; spark-doctor flags util ≥80% with power ≤25 W (`rules/power.py:12-26` @ ab08155); Sggin1's preflight requires ≥1,400 MHz and ≥40 W mean for ≥30 s and a since-boot SW power-cap counter that does not re-accumulate after a drain.
- [Code-verified] A fleet dashboard whose power and throttle fields are partly modelled. The own dashboard falls back to a hard-coded 120 W "limit" because GB10 reports N/A. Its "system power" is GPU draw plus a CPU utilisation model plus a constant 20 W. Its throttle state is four instantaneous flags, and its clock percentage is computed against 3,003 MHz, so a healthy stock box reads ~80%. None of this sees ms-scale clamps.
- [Historical diagnostic] Claiming `nvidia-smi` on GB10 has no power telemetry. GPU power draw (average and instantaneous) is exposed. Module power, memory power and the limits are not.
- [Community-measured] Capping only the head node of a TP group. TP ranks run in lockstep, so a head-only cap "measures nothing" (peter.h177).
- [Interpretation] Installing a cap unit with `daemon-reload` while engines run. Per the NVIDIA Container Toolkit doc, containers on the legacy injection path can lose NVML. No GB10 report either confirms or rules out this failure on current runc and driver versions.
- [Community-measured] Blaming every power-off on heat. Counter-cases:
  - failures at GPU 45-52 °C that followed the chassis across PSU swaps (forum 363370 #204);
  - halts only when hot AND memory-tight (forum 383624 #9);
  - OOM-driven hangs (forum 358280 #21; forum 377044 #14, which a moderator classed as OOM);
  - an own head-node wedge whose recorded signature is memory exhaustion, not heat (see [Memory on unified memory](11-memory-on-unified-memory.md)).

  Run the NVIDIA field diagnostic before blaming a runtime: PowerStress fails reproducibly at ~8 min (`MODS-020000600139`) on units that hard-lock while GpuStress passes, and a moderator approved an RMA on such a failure (forum 377478 #19-20).
- [Community-measured] Trusting one ACPI zone reading. Two zones swapped values (81.4 <-> 68.0 °C over ~3 s) with no load change, and that unit later failed PowerStress on thermal-sensor grounds (forum 377365 #2).
- [Community-measured] Assuming a GPU cap cools the CPU and SoC. The reports conflict:
  - it did not on one rack: head SoC 90-96 °C at every clock, and the GPU itself only 63 -> 56 °C across 2400-400 MHz (peter.h177, forum 379389 #1);
  - it did elsewhere: 96.0 -> 91.1 °C (ai-muninn) and CPU 93 -> 78 °C (whpthomas);
  - some owners report the GPU cap "starved" the CPU of cooling, because the fan curve follows GPU-side sensors (forum 381549 #1; forum 383821 #2), while azampatti disputes this.

  The workload decides. Check CPU zones separately.
- [Community-measured] Running DCGM diagnostics on GB10. With DCGM 3.3.9 (the latest in the SBSA repository at the time), `dcgmi diag` failed with "Detected unsupported Cuda version" on CUDA 13 (forum 376103 #12).

## 8. Open questions

- Mismatched caps across TP ranks. No source measures group throughput with one rank capped and another stock, or at different caps. One repo claims "leaving one node capped costs all three 16%" without a measurement (§7), and the latch evidence shows slow ranks pacing a group but is not a cap. Settle it with a TP2/TP3 A/B with arms {all stock, all 2100, head 2100 + workers stock, workers 2100 + head stock}: decode n≥20 per arm, cold prefill n≥5, counters diffed per rank.
- How long individual clamps last. Nobody has measured the duration of single SW-power-cap or HW-thermal clamps on GB10. The claim that the power-cap counter is made of "millisecond clamps" is [Interpretation]. The firmware power loop runs at 100 ms, and clamps have been missed at 100 ms and at 1 s (§7). Settle it with NVML clock-event-reason polling at 10-100 ms during a 250K prefill, alongside the cumulative counters, and, on a non-production node only, the unsupported SPBM channels (`pl1`, `syspl1`, `prochot`, energy accumulators) at 100 ms.
- Lock survival. The driver-reload half is answered at vendor-doc level: NVML says locks return to default "after system reboot or driver reload", and GB10 users confirm that reboots clear them. Still open on GB10: whether an `nvidia-persistenced` restart alone clears a lock, and whether a boot unit (with or without the device-wait `ExecStartPre`) applies before engines start. Settle it by restarting persistenced on an idle node and reading `clocks.current.sm` under a short load; then reboot one node with approval and check the loaded clock before any engine starts.
- The first-request-slow effect after applying a new lock (own sweep, three occurrences). An external TP4 fleet records a slow first request after ~13 min idle (94 vs 63 ms/step) and a first-use cost after boot, and the idle effect did not recur after a power cycle on that fleet. Nothing ties it to a newly applied lock, and the own packet already guessed "possibly first-request effect after idle".
