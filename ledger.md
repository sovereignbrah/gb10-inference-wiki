# Levers ledger

Each row below is one lever: a change an operator can make to a GB10 serving lane (ASUS Ascent GX10, NVIDIA GB10, 128 GB unified memory, one to three nodes over direct RoCE) to buy speed, capacity, stability or measurement quality. The row states the mechanism, where it applies as engine x model family x topology, the gain band with its evidence grade, what it costs, what can go wrong, and the topic pages that argue it. The ledger was assembled from section 5 of all 22 topic pages; where two or more pages state the same lever it appears once, with every page linked, so 251 page-level levers collapse to 156 rows. Nothing here adds up: levers interact through acceptance, row counts and memory, and each row's smallest falsifying experiment lives on its topic page.

Grades follow the program's evidence vocabulary: [Measured] is an own measurement cited by packet date and title, [Community-measured] is someone else's measurement, [Code-verified] is present in source at a pin but not necessarily GPU-validated, [Historical diagnostic] and [Interpretation] are retained records or explanations, and [Proposed] implies no gain. Rows are sorted by the strongest grade that carries the headline gain (rows 1-58 own-measured; rows 59-126 community-measured; rows 127-128 code-verified; rows 129-132 historical diagnostic or interpretation; rows 133-156 proposed), and within a grade by headline gain, largest first, with speed and latency levers ahead of correctness, capacity, stability and tooling levers of the same grade. A gain band marked 'not GB10' was measured on other hardware and is context, not a prediction; a bundle result is never a single lever's gain.
